# Music-distribution landing-page reference review

**Research date:** 24 October 2026

The six official sites reviewed—DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby, UnitedMasters, Amuse, and LANDR—share a practical conversion rhythm: explain the distribution offer near the top, show recognizable listening destinations, make the next step visible, answer basic artist questions, and repeat a clear call to action as visitors scroll. Their exact product terms and design systems differ, so these are broad UX patterns, not a template to copy. [1] [4] [7] [9] [12] [15]

DistroKid presents a short distribution promise, destination marks, product links, FAQs, and signup prompts. TuneCore also uses a strong hero and destination marks, then layers in service information, plans, FAQs, and signup routes. Their exact headline, logo treatment, gradient CTA and plan presentation are distinctive and should not be reproduced. [1] [2] [4] [5]

CD Baby makes the explanation more educational: it describes the service, lays out a three-step release journey, and links the process to FAQs and conversion actions. UnitedMasters uses artist-centered imagery, service information, proof sections, a process summary, and FAQs. The layout patterns can inform hierarchy, but its campaign photography, claims, and branded composition should remain its own. [7] [8] [9] [10]

Amuse separates distribution from artist-and-label services, then uses benefit sections, release steps, FAQs, and repeated account-start actions. LANDR foregrounds a concise distribution offer and CTA, followed by support and feature explanations. Their slogans, service promises, distinctive palettes, imagery, badges, and branded controls are not appropriate to reuse for MONTYX. [12] [13] [14] [15] [16]

## Design implications for MONTYX

A distinctive MONTYX page can borrow only the category-level sequence: a clear offer, destinations, a plain-language process, carefully selected benefits, FAQs, and a contact action. The proposed visual direction is an editorial “signal atlas”: MONTYX’s existing near-black, off-white and signal-crimson identity, angular wordmark, and an original abstract 3D distribution object with destination nodes. The animation should be progressive and decorative; the same message must remain available in normal HTML with a static fallback, mobile support, and reduced-motion behavior.

The user says the platform distributes to every popular music service and more. A small, readable grid of official service marks can identify example destinations, with a trademark/no-endorsement note and a caveat that availability can vary. Before making an exhaustive or unconditional coverage claim, the actual supported catalog and brand-use requirements should be checked. No logos should be presented as sponsors, partners, or certifications.

No MONTYX pricing, royalty share, payment schedule, release turnaround, analytics, named artists, statistics, testimonials, partner relationships, or guarantees were supplied. These should be omitted rather than borrowed or invented. The main conversion action should use the existing contact form until a real distribution signup flow is confirmed.
